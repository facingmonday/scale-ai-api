## **Week 1 – Baseline Operations**

**Scenario**
The course begins under calm, predictable conditions designed to mirror a “normal” operating environment. Customer demand follows expected patterns, suppliers are reliable, costs are stable, and there are no unusual disruptions in the market. At first glance, the business appears easy to manage. However, this stability removes all external excuses. Every result this week will be driven entirely by internal decisions. Inventory must be ordered without fear of delays, staffing can be scheduled with confidence, and pricing decisions will immediately reveal whether the business understands its own unit economics. This week establishes the operational rhythm that future disruptions will test.

### **Poor Outcome**

Even under calm, predictable conditions, the business struggled to find its footing. Sales underperformed due to misaligned pricing, while inventory either piled up unsold or ran out at the worst times. Staffing levels failed to match actual demand, leading to idle labor during slow periods and service bottlenecks during peak hours. Vendors performed as expected, but internal inefficiencies caused cash to steadily leak away. By the end of the week, the operation felt unstable despite the absence of any external disruption, exposing weak fundamentals early.

### **Average Outcome**

The business operated smoothly enough to stay afloat, but cracks were visible beneath the surface. Sales met expectations, yet inventory turnover was slower than ideal, tying up cash unnecessarily. Staffing schedules were functional but inefficient, with moments of overstaffing and mild service strain. Supplier relationships remained stable, and no major problems emerged, but the business failed to generate meaningful momentum. The week closed with modest results and the sense that better decisions could have produced stronger outcomes.

### **Exceptional Outcome**

Operations ran cleanly and confidently throughout the week. Pricing aligned well with demand, inventory flowed efficiently, and staffing levels matched customer traffic with minimal waste. Vendors delivered reliably, allowing the business to operate without friction. Sales translated into healthy margins and growing cash reserves, creating a strong operational rhythm. This week established trust in the system and positioned the business as resilient before challenges emerged.

## **Week 2 – Demand Forecasting Variability**

**Scenario**
Subtle shifts begin to appear in customer behavior. Demand no longer follows a perfectly predictable pattern and instead fluctuates modestly from day to day. Some periods see higher-than-expected traffic, while others are quieter than forecasted. These changes are not extreme, but they introduce uncertainty into planning. Inventory orders placed too early or too aggressively risk sitting idle, while cautious decisions may leave money on the table during demand spikes. Staffing decisions now carry more risk, as schedules must be set before demand is fully known. This week introduces the reality that forecasts are estimates, not guarantees.

### **Poor Outcome**

Unexpected swings in demand quickly exposed fragile forecasting assumptions. Inventory orders missed the mark, leaving shelves empty during spikes and overstocked during lulls. Staffing schedules failed to adapt, causing rushed service during busy periods and costly idle time afterward. Sales volatility strained cash flow, and the business struggled to respond in real time. Vendors delivered on time, but poor internal planning meant those deliveries often arrived too early or too late to be useful.

### **Average Outcome**

Demand fluctuations caused operational discomfort but not collapse. Some inventory was wasted, and a portion of demand went unmet due to cautious ordering. Staffing mismatches created occasional bottlenecks, but service levels remained acceptable. Vendor performance stayed consistent, allowing the business to recover quickly from missteps. The week ended with stable but uneven results, reinforcing how forecasting accuracy influences nearly every operational lever.

### **Exceptional Outcome**

The business anticipated variability and planned accordingly. Inventory levels flexed smoothly with demand, and staffing schedules absorbed fluctuations without service breakdowns. Sales remained strong even when demand shifted, and cash flow stayed healthy. Vendor deliveries aligned well with adjusted needs, preventing excess stock or shortages. The business demonstrated adaptive planning and earned operational confidence heading into more complex scenarios.

## **Week 3 – Inventory Management Pressure**

**Scenario**
Inventory begins to assert itself as a constraint rather than a neutral asset. Storage limits, spoilage, handling effort, or carrying costs now matter in meaningful ways. Excess inventory ties up cash and creates operational drag, while insufficient inventory leads to missed sales and frustrated customers. Vendor deliveries continue to arrive, but managing when and how much to order becomes increasingly consequential. Decisions made earlier in the week ripple forward, affecting staffing efficiency, space utilization, and flexibility. This scenario forces students to confront the cost of inventory beyond its purchase price.

### **Poor Outcome**

Inventory decisions became a major liability. Excess stock accumulated, tying up cash and increasing waste, while critical items still ran out during high-demand periods. Staffing struggled to manage inefficient inventory flow, compounding service issues. Vendors continued to deliver, but storage limits and spoilage eroded value quickly. Sales stagnated as availability and service reliability declined, leaving the business cash-constrained and operationally frustrated.

### **Average Outcome**

Inventory levels were workable but inefficient. Some cash sat idle in slow-moving stock, and occasional stockouts limited sales potential. Staffing adapted, but time was lost managing inventory instead of serving customers. Vendors remained dependable, helping prevent major disruptions. The business survived the week with acceptable performance but clear signals that inventory discipline needed improvement.

### **Exceptional Outcome**

Inventory flowed with precision. Orders matched demand closely, minimizing waste while maintaining high availability. Staffing remained focused on service rather than problem-solving, and vendor deliveries integrated seamlessly into operations. Sales converted efficiently into cash, and inventory became a strategic asset rather than a burden. The business exited the week lean, flexible, and confident.

## **Week 4 – Labor Constraints**

**Scenario**
Labor availability tightens as staffing becomes more difficult or expensive. Businesses must operate within narrower labor limits while still meeting customer demand and production targets. Efficiency and scheduling discipline become more important.

### **Poor Outcome**

The labor environment tightens. Staffing is no longer infinitely flexible, and the cost of labor becomes more visible. Scheduling too many workers drains cash quickly, while scheduling too few creates service bottlenecks, longer wait times, and missed sales. Fatigue, morale, and productivity begin to matter as operations stretch to meet demand with fewer hands. Inventory may be available, and vendors may deliver on time, but without the right staffing levels, throughput becomes the limiting factor. This week highlights labor as both a cost center and a capacity constraint.

### **Average Outcome**

Labor constraints created friction but were manageable. Staffing covered essential needs, though efficiency suffered. Some sales were lost during busy windows, and labor costs crept higher than planned. Inventory and vendors remained stable, preventing larger failures. The week ended with steady but strained performance.

### **Exceptional Outcome**

Labor was allocated with discipline and foresight. Staffing levels flexed with demand, preserving service quality while controlling costs. Inventory moved smoothly, and vendor coordination remained strong. Sales held steady or improved despite constraints, reinforcing that labor planning is a critical driver of throughput and profitability.

## **Week 6 – Input Cost Volatility**

**Scenario**
Costs that were once stable begin to fluctuate unexpectedly. Key inputs increase in price, sometimes with little warning, compressing margins and increasing financial risk. Inventory purchased this week may cost significantly more than inventory purchased last week, forcing businesses to rethink pricing, output, or volume strategies. Vendor invoices rise faster than anticipated, and cash outflows accelerate. Decisions now require balancing customer price sensitivity against the need to protect margins. This scenario highlights how cost volatility can destabilize even well-run operations.

### **Poor Outcome**

Rising input costs crushed margins. Inventory purchased at higher prices moved slowly, while sales revenue failed to keep pace. Staffing costs compounded losses, and vendor invoices drained cash faster than expected. Attempts to maintain volume without adjusting strategy accelerated financial decline. The business exited the week weakened and reactive.

### **Average Outcome**

Cost increases reduced profitability, but the business remained operational. Pricing and production adjustments came late, limiting their effectiveness. Inventory costs rose, but staffing and vendor relationships stayed manageable. Cash flow tightened, signaling vulnerability but not failure.

### **Exceptional Outcome**

The business responded decisively to cost volatility. Pricing and output adjusted quickly, preserving margins without collapsing demand. Inventory purchases were disciplined, staffing remained efficient, and vendor relationships stayed stable. Cash flow absorbed the shock, reinforcing operational resilience.

## **Week 7 – Midterm Stress Test**

**Scenario**
Multiple pressures converge at once. Demand is less predictable, costs remain volatile, labor is constrained, and supplier reliability is uneven. None of these challenges are extreme on their own, but together they create a stressful operating environment where small mistakes compound quickly. Inventory decisions interact with staffing availability, vendor delays disrupt production timing, and cash flow becomes more sensitive to short-term shocks. This scenario is designed to expose weak links in the system and reward businesses that have built resilient, balanced operations.

### **Poor Outcome**

Multiple pressures converged and overwhelmed operations. Demand shifts, rising costs, and labor constraints compounded existing inefficiencies. Inventory misalignment worsened, vendors became harder to manage, and staffing morale declined. Sales volatility and mounting costs drained cash rapidly. The system buckled under cumulative strain.

### **Average Outcome**

The business absorbed overlapping challenges unevenly. Some systems held while others strained. Inventory and staffing adjustments reduced losses but could not fully stabilize performance. Vendor coordination prevented collapse, but momentum slowed noticeably. The week revealed structural weaknesses without complete failure.

### **Exceptional Outcome**

Strong fundamentals carried the business through compounded stress. Inventory, staffing, and vendor coordination remained synchronized despite pressure. Sales softened but remained profitable, and cash reserves held firm. This week proved the system could withstand real-world complexity.

## **Week 8 – Capacity Utilization Decisions**

**Scenario**
Attention shifts toward how aggressively the business chooses to operate. Running near maximum capacity offers the promise of higher revenue but increases strain on labor, inventory, and systems. Operating conservatively preserves stability but may leave demand unmet. Vendors can support higher volumes, but only if orders are timed correctly. Staffing must absorb higher throughput without collapsing efficiency. This scenario forces students to make explicit choices about growth, risk tolerance, and operational intensity.

### **Poor Outcome**

Overextending capacity strained labor, increased waste, and degraded service quality. Alternatively, underutilization left inventory idle and sales unrealized. Vendors delivered as planned, but internal misalignment squandered opportunity. Cash flow suffered as costs outpaced gains.

### **Average Outcome**

Capacity decisions balanced caution and ambition imperfectly. Some growth was captured, but inefficiencies reduced profitability. Staffing and inventory absorbed moderate strain, and vendors remained steady. Performance was stable but lacked momentum.

### **Exceptional Outcome**

Capacity was leveraged intelligently. Staffing scaled smoothly, inventory turned efficiently, and vendors supported increased throughput. Sales growth translated into real profit, strengthening cash reserves and operational confidence.

## **Week 9 – Demand Surge**

**Scenario**
An external event triggers a temporary surge in customer demand. The increase is real but short-lived, and its duration is uncertain. Businesses that are prepared can capture significant upside, while those that are not may experience chaos rather than success. Inventory may sell out faster than expected, staffing may be overwhelmed, and vendors may struggle to respond quickly. Overreacting can be just as damaging as underreacting, leaving businesses with excess inventory once demand normalizes. This scenario tests the ability to scale quickly without losing control.

**Scenario Output**
Businesses that anticipated the surge and prepared inventory and labor captured significant upside. Others were constrained by capacity and missed sales. Some overreacted and incurred unnecessary costs. This week illustrates the challenges of scaling quickly under uncertainty.

## **Week 10 – Price Sensitivity Effects**

**Scenario**
Customer behavior becomes more reactive to pricing decisions. Small changes in price now lead to noticeable shifts in demand, either driving customers away or increasing volume without guaranteeing profitability. Competitive alternatives influence purchasing decisions more strongly, and perceived value plays a larger role. Inventory turnover, staffing needs, and vendor orders all respond to pricing signals. This scenario emphasizes that pricing is not just a financial decision, but a demand-shaping lever with cascading operational consequences.

### **Poor Outcome**

Pricing changes triggered sharp customer reactions. Sales volume dropped suddenly or increased without profitability. Inventory accumulated or drained unpredictably, staffing schedules fell out of sync, and vendor purchases misaligned with demand. Cash flow deteriorated as pricing assumptions proved flawed.

### **Average Outcome**

Pricing adjustments produced mixed signals. Demand shifted, but margins failed to improve meaningfully. Inventory and staffing absorbed the changes without major disruption, but the business failed to unlock strategic advantage.

### **Exceptional Outcome**

Pricing aligned precisely with customer behavior. Sales volume and margins balanced effectively, inventory turnover improved, staffing stabilized, and vendor orders matched real demand. Cash flow strengthened as pricing became a strategic lever rather than a risk.

**Scenario**
Uncertainty increases as the possibility of unexpected negative events looms. These events may affect costs, demand, or operations without warning. Businesses with thin cash reserves face difficult tradeoffs, while those with stronger liquidity have more room to maneuver. Inventory purchases, staffing commitments, and vendor payments must be evaluated through the lens of risk rather than growth alone. This scenario focuses attention on liquidity, downside protection, and the ability to survive shocks rather than optimize for best-case outcomes.

### **Poor Outcome**

Unexpected setbacks hit when cash reserves were thin. Inventory purchases and staffing costs quickly became liabilities, forcing reactive cuts and missed sales. Vendor payments strained relationships, and operational confidence declined sharply.

### **Average Outcome**

Cash reserves absorbed some shocks, but flexibility was limited. Inventory and staffing decisions became cautious, slowing growth. Vendor relationships remained intact, though strained. The business survived but operated defensively.

### **Exceptional Outcome**

Strong liquidity provided breathing room. Inventory, staffing, and vendor relationships remained stable despite uncertainty. The business absorbed setbacks calmly and preserved momentum, proving the value of disciplined cash management.
